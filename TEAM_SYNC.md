  collectibles, but still skews toward people who care about fashion — not truly universal.
  Worked through real safety/parent-trust concerns for it (school-verified accounts only, no
  stranger meetups, safe drop points instead of 1:1 handoffs, in-app-only chat, optional parent
  visibility) in case swap-matching comes back as a mechanic for a different, broader category.
- **Group-project fairness tracker** / **anonymous real-time doubt-clearing button for class** —
  genuinely universal among students, but still capped at "students" as the customer base, and
  didn't survive the bigger test below.

## The filter we landed on (apply this to any new idea)

Two questions kill most ideas fast:
1. **"Would someone just ask ChatGPT/Google instead?"** — if the product is fundamentally
   answering a question or explaining something, it's dead; a chatbot already does that for
   free. Only ideas that need a real physical action, a real trusted human nearby, or moving/
   lending an actual object survive this.
2. **"Is the customer base bigger than just students?"** — students are a fraction of the total
   market; adults are fair game too, and judges respond well to a team that isn't just building
   for its own age bracket.

Ideas floated that pass both filters (not yet fully vetted, worth digging into):
- **Structured "society WhatsApp group"** — organizing the informal borrow/lend/local-service-
  recommendation behavior that already happens in every neighborhood/apartment complex into a
  searchable, verified-address app instead of buried group chats. Good validation hook: survey
  adults on whether they've ever borrowed something or asked for a service recommendation in a
  local group chat — expect near-unanimous yes, which is real data for the Problem Validation
  phase.
- **Instant hyperlocal help for small physical tasks** (fix a fan, assemble furniture, carry
  something upstairs) — UrbanCompany-adjacent, differentiated by being neighbor-to-neighbor and
  for tasks too small/urgent for a booked professional.
