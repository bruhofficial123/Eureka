# Idea Bank — Eureka! Junior

Companion to `TEAM_SYNC.md`. Every idea generated in Aniket's Claude session, with the reasoning
and the verdict for each. Dead ends are kept on purpose so nobody re-suggests them.

## Submission brief (official wording, for reference)

Deliverable: Survey Analysis Report / Presentation, PDF, **max 5 pages**.
Required sections: Problem Area, Survey Questions, Summary of Responses, Key Insights/Core
Problem, detailed Solution Description.
Survey: **10+ relevant individuals**, self-designed, short and focused.
Stated emphasis: *"data-driven problem definition and solution alignment."*

Two constraints follow from the exact wording, and both affect which idea we pick:

1. **"Relevant individuals"** — respondents must credibly sit inside the affected population,
   and the report has to justify the sample. This makes *how easily we can reach 10 real
   respondents* an idea-selection criterion, not an afterthought.
2. **"Solution alignment"** — the solution must visibly fall out of the data. So survey
   questions must probe **past behaviour and current workarounds**, never "would you use an app
   that...". Opinions prove nothing; behaviour forces the solution.

## Suggested page map

| Page | Required section | What goes on it |
|------|------------------|-----------------|
| 1 | Problem Area | The problem, who it hits, why now |
| 2 | Survey Questions | The questions **and** the methodology — who we asked, how many, why they qualify as relevant |
| 3 | Summary of Responses | Charts and actual numbers, not prose |
| 4 | Key Insights / Core Problem | Synthesis into one sharp problem statement |
| 5 | Solution Description | Each feature traced back to a page-4 insight |

Most teams lose marks on page 2 by listing questions without justifying the sample. State it
explicitly.

## Filter (extends the two questions in TEAM_SYNC.md)

Original two, unchanged:

1. Would someone just ask ChatGPT/Google instead?
2. Is the customer base bigger than just students?

**Third axis added this session — the forced event.** Applying question 1 on its own funnels
every idea into "rent or lend a physical object", which is the most crowded and least original
category available. That is exactly what happened in Round 1 below. The correction: prefer
problems attached to an event people are *forced* into, on a deadline, with a financial or legal
penalty for being unprepared. Nobody churns out of an obligation.

**Fourth, practical: can we reach 10 relevant respondents this week?** Several otherwise-decent
ideas die here, and it is the binding constraint on the current deadline.

## Round 1 — object-lending shapes (all rejected)

Verdict on the batch: four rental marketplaces in different clothes. Two already exist as real
businesses. Recorded so they are not re-proposed.

### 1.1 Kid-gear pass-along
Prams, cribs, car seats, high chairs, cycles — used 8-14 months, then dead weight in a loft.
The argument was that obsolescence here is forced by biology rather than taste, which was meant
to fix the "too niche" objection that killed the clothes-swap idea.
**Rejected:** functionally OLX / Facebook Marketplace. Used kid gear already sells there and the
swap-matching veneer is thin.

### 1.2 Post-discharge medical equipment
Wheelchairs, walkers, hospital beds, oxygen concentrators — bought for a few weeks at high cost,
then stored for years.
**Rejected:** rental services for this already exist commercially. Also fails the sampling test —
finding 10 families who bought equipment *recently* is hard without a hospital contact.

### 1.3 Function-item lending
Serving dishes for 60, folding chairs, decor and lights for weddings, pujas, birthdays.
**Rejected:** this is the tent-house business. Every locality in India already has one.

### 1.4 Eyes-on-my-parents
A verified neighbour who physically checks in on elderly parents living in a different city from
their adult children. Presence as a product, not healthcare — the purest form of filter 1, since
it is valuable precisely because a human body has to be in the room.
**Rejected:** Emoha, Khyaal and Samarth already do this. Worth retaining the underlying insight
if a fresh angle on it appears.

## Round 2 — forced-event shapes (stronger; none are marketplaces)

### 2.1 Move-out deposit disputes — current front-runner
A tenant moves out and the landlord withholds a large chunk of the deposit citing damage. The
tenant has no proof of what the place looked like at move-in, so they absorb the loss.
The product is **evidence, not a marketplace**: a timestamped, structured condition record made
at move-in and signed off by both sides, so the move-out argument runs against a record instead
of against memory.

- Forced event, hard deadline, real money at stake
- Far bigger than students — India's entire urban renting population
- Respondents: anyone who has ever rented. Ten are reachable in an afternoon and nearly all of
  them have a specific grievance story
- Not ChatGPT-able: requires physically documenting a real room at a specific moment

### 2.2 Wedding vendor advances
Families hand 50-70% upfront to caterers, decorators and photographers. Some underdeliver, some
vanish. Lakhs of rupees, no recourse, and the date cannot be moved.
The product is **staged release against delivery milestones** — de-risking money that is already
committed, rather than matching families to vendors, which is done to death.

- Immovable deadline, very large sums
- Respondents: anyone whose family has hosted a wedding
- Risk: reads as fintech. Keep the pitch concrete

### 2.3 Maintenance history for used goods
Buying a second-hand bike, AC, laptop or phone in India is blind. No service record exists and no
seller claim is verifiable.
The product is a **service history that attaches to the object**, built from the repair shops
already doing the work.

- Respondents: anyone who has bought or sold something used
- Risk: chicken-and-egg. The record has no value until repair shops participate. Say so openly
  if this one is chosen

## Where this stands

Nothing is locked. The recommendation is **2.1, move-out deposits** — chosen on respondent
density as much as on the idea itself. The brief's "10+ relevant individuals" is the binding
constraint this week, and renters are the easiest relevant population to reach in volume, with
the most specific complaints.

Everything downstream — survey design, collecting 10+ responses, drafting the 5-page PDF — is
blocked until the idea is locked.
